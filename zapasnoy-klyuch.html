Spare Key

I designed a phone prototype for a parent who is stuck on an error screen and would rather call than tap. The screenshot is read on the device. Nothing is uploaded.

Role

Sole product designer

Platform

Mobile web prototype

Timeline

2026, concept

Reach

Not shipped

Result

Error screen named on the device

The problem

The call happens before the tap

A parent cannot get into an account. She mixes up services. If the Apple ID uses a Gmail address, she treats them as the same login. While she waits for someone to pick up, the fear of breaking the phone gets louder than the error itself.

The old fix was a person on the other end: TeamViewer, screenshots, an explanation at the kitchen table. She was embarrassed to ask what she called a stupid question.

The job is not password recovery. It is one safe tap she can make alone.

What I found

Shame and a wrong mental model

Interviews around this job kept returning the same three lines. I did not turn them into a persona. I used them as constraints.

If Apple ID signs in with her Gmail, she can believe it is Gmail. She is afraid of pressing the wrong thing and breaking the phone. She is embarrassed to ask her son a stupid question.

Design decisions

What I refused to build

1. The photo stays on the phone

Recognition runs in the browser. The image is not sent to an account, and the full text the reader saw is not shown back. A password in the frame would otherwise become a second copy.

2. Two services means say so

When the screen contains both Apple ID and Gmail, the prototype does not pick a winner and sound sure. It says both words are there. That is the confusion the product exists for.

3. Green is the only thing you press

The visual system is the paper page from the concept, not an agency site. Ochre is the name. The serif is the headline. Forest green is reserved for the one next step. Alarm red never becomes the brand.

What this is not

A working read, not a launched product

I checked the reader against three fixture screens: Apple ID with a wrong password, Google asking for a code, and WhatsApp asking for a phone number. All three were named correctly, in the browser, without uploading the image.

Google, WhatsApp, Facebook and Gosuslugi stop at a named screen and a warning. They do not yet have a step-by-step plan. I am not going to invent a completion rate for a prototype nobody has used alone.

What I learned

Detection is the cheap part

Naming the logo does not make her press the next control. The design has to show which words it trusted, admit when two services are on the same screen, and then offer one action that cannot delete her photos.

The references I like for a portfolio site are the wrong material for this. Giant type and acid yellow would make the tool look like a studio. She needs paper, a large sentence, and a button that means go.
